On Wednesday evenings, Providence Road youth sit down with some of the most demanding texts in the American literary canon — and they don't pretend to understand them. That's the point. The Providence Road book club is built on a counterintuitive premise: confusion isn't a failure to be hidden. It's the spark that ignites real learning. When a thirteen-year-old looks at a passage from *The Narrative of Frederick Douglass* and says out loud, *"This doesn't make sense!"* — that's not a problem. That's the most important thing he's said all week. The book club creates the space where that moment is safe, celebrated, and followed through.

## The Pedagogy: Making Confusion Safe

Most educational environments — in and out of school — implicitly punish not knowing. Students learn early to perform comprehension rather than pursue it. The book club disrupts that pattern.

The approach mirrors what Providence Road calls the **Chasing Understanding®** methodology: notice the moment you don't understand, name it out loud, and stay in the struggle long enough to break through. The breakthrough is not just cognitive — it's a transformation in how a young person sees himself relative to knowledge and difficulty.

> *"This doesn't make sense!"* Kyree's exclamation captures the struggle many youths in North Baton Rouge face when they want to understand, but few have the space or are comfortable enough to make such a statement.

That statement — *"This doesn't make sense!"* — is the beginning of learning, not evidence of failure. When youth in the book club start saying it freely, something has shifted. The spark of curiosity that says *"what does this mean"* is the same spark that unlocks the capacity of the human spirit and awakens a desire to step into the unknown.

## A Surge in Confidence

Since the book club's academic journey began in 2021, the most striking outcome has not been test scores or reading levels. It has been confidence. Through dedicated practice, youth members started acknowledging their own aptitude to grasp difficult concepts and communicate eloquently in writing.

The summer of 2023 marked a turning point. Youth who had once avoided the page were leaning in — staying in the confusion, asking questions, and discovering that they could find the answer. The confidence they built in the book club began showing up at home, at school, and in the community.

## Kyree's Poem

The best evidence of the book club's impact is the writing it produces. Below is a poem written by Kyree, age 13, during a Wednesday book club session on July 19, 2023. It emerged from a summer of wrestling with hard texts, learning to name confusion, and discovering — one word at a time — that understanding is something you can chase.

<Tip>
  Read this poem as a window into what happens when a young person is given the space to be honest about struggle and the tools to work through it.
</Tip>

---

*Worthy of happening, working towards success.*\
*Way over the horizon working to be the best.*\
*Steppin with affirmation and mastering it along the way.*\
*Gaining wisdom that would help me in a tremendous way.*\
*Creating healthy boundaries that won't leave my side.*\
*Bringing happiness into my life no more darkness ever seen.*\
*Dreams don't happen unless you do.*\
*Being confident that my life would be great*\
*leaving all the dark thoughts behind.*\
*Slowly being fueled up by what God wants Kyree to see,*\
*life will be peaceful as long as you walk with me.*\
*Face challenges and work with me.*\
*and be in the biggest picture the world has ever seen.*

**— Kyree, age 13 (July 19, 2023)**
