Behind every Providence Road program, every mentorship conversation, and every moment of structured growth is a single repeatable framework: the NVITES Arc™. Developed by Coach J' and refined through years of direct work with youth in North Baton Rouge, the NVITES Arc™ is a six-step sequence that teaches young people — and the adults who work with them — how to pay attention to what is actually happening, take it seriously, engage with it honestly, and pass the learning forward. It is simple enough to use in a ten-minute session and deep enough to reshape how a young person moves through every relationship in their life.

The arc appears across Providence Road's two structured programs — Big Steps™ and Chasing Understanding® — but it is equally present in one-on-one mentorship and the everyday rhythms of a service project. Once you understand NVITES, you begin to see it everywhere Providence Road shows up.

<Tip>
  You don't have to use the full arc every time. Even practicing just "Notice" and "Value" creates measurable change in 2–4 weeks — more intentional attention, fewer dismissed moments, and a noticeably different tone in how youth talk about themselves and each other.
</Tip>

## The Six Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="N — Notice">
    **Name the moment.** Something real just happened — a first-time effort, a moment of confusion, a choice that could have gone either way but didn't. The NVITES Arc begins with the discipline of actually seeing it. Most moments pass unacknowledged in environments where survival takes priority over reflection. Notice interrupts that pattern. It trains youth to slow down, look clearly at what just occurred, and put language to it before it disappears. Notice is the foundation everything else rests on: you cannot value, inquire about, or trade what you haven't first seen.
  </Step>

  <Step title="V — Value">
    **This matters. Say so.** Pausing is not enough — the moment must be acknowledged as significant. Value is the step where youth and adults practice the discipline of treating a real effort, a real struggle, or a real breakthrough as worth stopping for. In environments where showing that something matters makes you vulnerable, this step is countercultural. It trains the capacity to care out loud — to communicate to another person that what they did or experienced deserves attention. Value is what makes the arc feel safe rather than performative.
  </Step>

  <Step title="I — Inquire">
    **Ask better questions.** Once a moment is seen and valued, the next move is to get curious about it. Inquire invites youth to go deeper than the surface — to ask what actually happened, what it means, what led to it, and what it might lead to next. Good inquiry resists the pull toward easy answers. In Big Steps™, this looks like peers asking specific, authentic questions about a teammate's first-time action. In Chasing Understanding®, it looks like students articulating exactly where comprehension broke down and what question would fix it. Either way, Inquire builds the habit of precision over assumption.
  </Step>

  <Step title="T — Trade">
    **Exchange the shortcut for an honest attempt.** This is the pivot point of the arc. Every youth operating in survival mode has developed shortcuts: fake understanding, performed toughness, denial, deflection, and the thousand other ways of getting through a moment without actually engaging with it. Trade asks for something different — not perfection, but honesty. Give up the fake read for a real attempt. Give up the performance of knowing for a genuine question. Give up the armor of tough for one honest move toward strong. The trade doesn't have to be total or permanent; it just has to be real.
  </Step>

  <Step title="E — Enjoy">
    **Celebrate the partial win.** You don't have to have it all figured out for the moment to be worth celebrating. Enjoy is the step that rewires the relationship between effort and reward — teaching youth that making a genuine attempt, even an imperfect one, is cause for recognition. In a context where most celebrations are reserved for outcomes (winning, finishing, succeeding), Enjoy validates the process. It is what makes the arc sustainable: youth who experience genuine, specific affirmation for honest effort are far more likely to make another honest attempt than youth who are only praised when they get it exactly right.
  </Step>

  <Step title="S — Share">
    **What you learned can help someone else.** The arc closes with an outward turn. Share asks youth to take what they noticed, valued, inquired about, traded for, and enjoyed — and pass it on. This is the tough-to-strong move embedded directly in the framework: the growth you experienced is not just for you. It belongs to the community. When Kevin gave his front-seat reward to Young D, that was Share in its most distilled form. When a student in a Chasing Understanding® session tells the group what unlocked comprehension for them, they are making the whole group stronger. Share is where individual growth becomes cultural change.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The NVITES Arc™ in Providence Road's Programs

The arc is not abstract — it runs through two concrete, structured programs that any school, after-school program, or youth organization can implement.

## Why One Framework Across Everything

Using a single arc across programs is not an accident of convenience. It is a deliberate design choice rooted in how youth learn character. When a young person practices Notice in a book club and then hears the same language used on a service project and then sees it in a Big Steps™ circle, the arc stops being a technique and starts being a way of moving through the world. Repetition across contexts is how a practice becomes an identity.

The NVITES Arc™ also does something specific that matters deeply in Providence Road's context: it creates a shared vocabulary for experiences that would otherwise go unnamed. In environments where emotional expression is dangerous, having a word for "this moment matters" — and a safe, structured ritual for what to do next — gives youth a way to engage with their own growth without having to invent the language from scratch.
