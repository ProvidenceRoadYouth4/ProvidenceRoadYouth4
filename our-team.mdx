The people behind Providence Road are not program administrators running operations from a distance — they are the Jump Team, a group of determined individuals who show up on the ground, in the neighborhoods, week after week. They carry the belief that showing up for youth is never one-directional. As Coach J' puts it best:

> "We show up to serve youth, just to realize when we get down in it, that it's as much for our hearts as it is for theirs."
>
> — Coach J' (Jon Odenwald), Co-Founder & Executive Director

The Jump Team is both local and far-reaching, skilled and still learning, seasoned and always growing. What they share is a full commitment to Providence Road's vision and an unshakable belief that hard work in beautiful places changes lives — for the youth, and for themselves.

## Jon Odenwald — Coach J'

**Co-Founder & Executive Director**

Jon O's journey began twenty years ago with a question that refused to leave him alone: *"Where are the great men — those who show up strong for others?"* What he found then is still true today: there were too few of them. That observation did not become an excuse. It became a calling.

For years, Jon set out to identify, call out, and guide leaders who had not yet found their voice. In the fall of 2001, he led a group of college men on an adventure to West Virginia to raft a Class V river. That expedition lit something — the pursuit of challenge and genuine connection. Over the following years, the adventures gave way to service projects, and the circle of young men around Jon grew, deepened, and spread across the country. Today, those men are serving their families and communities from a place of knowing they were seen and affirmed in their strength.

In 2015, Jon met Cole Erno by chance. Cole was ready for the struggle. After years of traveling hard roads and sharing in the work together, the two men crystallized a shared vision that would become Providence Road. In 2018, with a small group of brave volunteers and supporters, Providence Road was launched in the streets of Baton Rouge with a simple but serious premise: change attitudes, change perspectives, change culture.

> "Every story has a hero. I'm not the hero; I find young heroes. With encouragement, they begin to step into strong, believing what is most true, and realize that their story is just beginning."
>
> — Coach J'

Jon is married to Sharon, whose unwavering support has made the entire journey possible. He has four adult children, all living big stories of their own, and all proud supporters of their dad and the work of Providence Road.

## Cole Erno

**Co-Founder**

Cole Erno was one of the first to pioneer the concept of Providence Road, and his fingerprints are on every part of what the program has become. Without his commitment and diligence to developing and implementing the program side of Providence Road, there would be no program.

Cole was shaped early by strong men: his grandfathers — a World War II veteran and a 45-year career railroad engineer — and his father, an industrial designer and engineer. From them, he inherited a heart to serve, serious grit, and a creative instinct to build something better than what currently exists.

As a co-founder, Cole steps courageously into a void that most people pass by. He builds relationships with youth who carry difficult stories, and he calls out the strength he sees in each of them — not the strength they perform for the world, but the real kind, the kind that costs something. Most days, you can find Cole and his wife Jennifer hosting the youth of Providence Road in their home, where the warmth and safety of a real family table does more than any curriculum can.

Cole is responsible for coining the phrase that has become Providence Road's defining motto:

*"Doing hard things in beautiful places changes lives."*

## Dwight Jenkins

**Youth Mentor and Volunteer**

Dwight Jenkins' story is one of the most powerful in the Providence Road family — and he would be the last to say so. Born and raised in the 16th Ward of New Orleans, Dwight has lived a life that holds both deep loss and remarkable transformation.

After returning home from over three decades at Angola State Penitentiary, Dwight did not retreat. He leaned forward. Since 2022, he has served as a dedicated youth mentor in North Baton Rouge, volunteering multiple nights a week to guide and support the next generation of young men. He knows what it looks like when a young person chooses the wrong road — and he shows up, with patience and specificity, to help them choose a different one.

Known affectionately as **"Country Boy,"** Dwight brings a calm presence, a strong work ethic, and a depth of life experience that no degree can replicate. His journey from incarceration to leadership — in community mentorship and in property management at Harvest Companies — is a living testimony to the dignity that comes from second chances and meaningful connection.

Providence Road is proud to have Dwight as one of its steadiest pillars.
