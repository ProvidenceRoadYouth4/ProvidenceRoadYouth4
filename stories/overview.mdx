Providence Road believes that transformation is not an abstraction — it shows up in the details: a kid who once settled every conflict with his fists learning to use his words, a volunteer who drove out to the neighborhood unsure whether anything could change and returning year after year certain that it can. Numbers tell part of the story — hundreds of service hours, tons of trash removed, meals shared — but the fullest evidence of what happens on this road is found in the people who have walked it. These are their accounts, told as honestly as we can tell them, because the people of North Baton Rouge deserve that kind of witness.

> "I just want to thank Providence Road. We are very thankful for people coming to bad neighborhoods to help. The love they show these guys in the group is heartwarming to people in this community."
>
> — Jarvis, age 14

Stories here are organized into two groups: youth whose lives have been changed by Providence Road's programs, and volunteers and community partners who found that showing up for others changed them just as deeply. Read one. Read all of them. Then consider how your own story might intersect with this one.
