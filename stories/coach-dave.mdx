Tucked just under North Blvd at 100 North 14th Street in Baton Rouge, there is a gym where Providence Road youth come on Thursday nights for boxing lessons with the BREC recreation program. Coach Dave has run that gym for years. When the Providence Road group first walked in over a year ago, he did not know what to expect. What he saw was a group of young men who were, by his account, "always getting into it with each other, fighting at the drop of a hat." What he sees today is something different enough that it has become the clearest testimony in his life — not an award, but a reward.

> "There are awards, and there are rewards. An award is something others give you because you did something meaningful to them. A reward is something you get to receive because it's meaningful to you. Seeing the change in these young guys is a huge reward for me!"
>
> — Coach Dave

## A Year of Difference

Coach Dave's story is a witness account. He is not the one delivering the program or writing the curriculum — he is the person in the gym, week after week, who has watched the transformation play out in real time. And the change he describes is not abstract.

*"When these guys come up and gather around me in the ring, talking calmly and using their words — that's a huge deal and a big difference from a year ago."*

From fighting at the drop of a hat to gathering calmly in the ring. A year of Providence Road's consistent presence in these young men's lives — service projects, meetings, relationships, and structure — has produced a camaraderie that now crosses from coach to student and back.

Coach Dave can say, with the authority of someone who was present for both chapters: these young men are not the same.

## "I Am These Kids"

What makes Coach Dave's witness particularly powerful is the line he shares about himself: *"I am these kids. I lived their story."*

His story and the stories of the Providence Road youth are not parallel lines — they are the same line, separated by years. That recognition is what brings him back to the gym each week. It is not pity or charity. It is identification. He sees in these young men a version of himself, and he shows up because he knows what it would have meant to have someone in his corner who understood.

The biggest difference between where Coach Dave ended up and where these youth might end up is not talent or intelligence or even circumstance — it is the presence of a community gathered around them. A team of volunteers, staff, and donors committed to seeing them succeed.

## The Battle of Showing Up

Coach Dave's story also speaks to the interior challenge that every volunteer at Providence Road faces — the one that happens before you ever arrive.

We tend to disqualify ourselves before we even get to the hill. The statements that rise up are familiar: *"I've seen this story before and it doesn't end well." "What difference can I actually make?" "Surely these youth need more than someone showing up once a week."*

Those doubts are real. Showing up in hard places, week after week, with no guarantee that anything is changing — that is a challenge to faith in the most direct sense. It is easy to stay home. It is easy to convince yourself that someone more qualified, someone with better resources or a bigger platform, should be the one in that gym on Thursday night.

But the beauty of what Providence Road reveals is that impact is felt on both sides. You show up to serve youth and discover, somewhere along the way, that you are receiving as much as you are giving. The camaraderie that now fills Coach Dave's boxing gym is not just for the youth in the ring — it belongs to everyone who has chosen to be present.

## Providence Road Starts with Us

The transformation happening in the boxing gym at 100 North 14th Street is not a program outcome. It is a community outcome — the result of adults choosing, week after week, to be present in a hard place. Coach Dave is one of those adults. His reward is watching young men learn to use their words, gather calmly, and grow into something stronger than they were a year ago.

That reward is available to anyone willing to show up.
