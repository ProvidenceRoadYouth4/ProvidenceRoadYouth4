Every day, youth in North Baton Rouge face difficult choices — and they face them with very limited options. The neighborhood shapes the menu. When the adults around you are surviving rather than thriving, when the street holds more visible authority than any institution that claims to serve you, the path of least resistance and the path of greatest danger often look like the same road. Tay grew up on that road. What changed his trajectory wasn't a program brochure or a one-time event — it was proximity, consistency, and relationship.

## The Reality

North Baton Rouge is not short on challenges. Youth in this community encounter environments where the pressure to conform to street culture is immediate and physical. The choice to step away from that culture is not simply a decision — it is a daily act of courage that requires something to step *toward*. Without a compelling alternative, without adults who show up consistently and without agenda, that courage rarely has anywhere to land.

For Tay, and for many youth like him, the absence of options is not an abstraction. It is the backdrop against which every Tuesday and Thursday afternoon unfolds. The question Providence Road asks is not whether these youth are capable of making better choices — they are. The question is whether anyone is present, week after week, to make those choices feel real and worth making.

## How Providence Road Enters the Picture

Providence Road doesn't arrive with a checklist of requirements. It shows up. Coach J', Cole Erno, and the Jump Team are present in the neighborhood — not behind a desk in an office building across town, but *here*, in the streets where Tay lives. That proximity is itself a message: you are worth showing up for.

Consistency deepens the message. The same adults, the same rhythm of service projects and conversations and shared meals, week after week. Over time, consistency builds what North Baton Rouge youth have often been denied: the experience of an adult who doesn't disappear.

Relationship is where the transformation takes root. Providence Road staff and volunteers share their own stories — the struggles, the detours, the places where their own paths could have gone differently. That vulnerability is not a program technique. It is an honest exchange that makes it possible for Tay to be honest in return, to admit the weight of where he came from, and to begin imagining where he might go.

## A Safe Space to Practice Different Choices

Providence Road doesn't ask youth to arrive already changed. It invites them to come as they are and practice something different in a space where it is safe to struggle. Service projects give Tay a concrete context to exercise self-discipline, cooperation, and effort — skills that the street doesn't reward but that Providence Road does. Book club and the Chasing Understanding® curriculum give him language for his interior life. The gym trips and the meals and the meetings give him a community that is pulling for him.

The invitation is simple: show up. Providence Road meets youth where they are, not where they ought to be. Tay's story is the story of what becomes possible when someone extends that kind of welcome and keeps extending it, one week at a time, until a young person starts to believe it is real.
