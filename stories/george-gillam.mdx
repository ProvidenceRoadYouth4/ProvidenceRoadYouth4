George Gillam is not an outside observer of the world Providence Road works in. He grew up inside it. He survived it — barely. And the unique authority he brings to the youth in Spanish Town is not the authority of a credential or a title, but the authority of someone who has lived the story these young men are living right now and can hold it up to the light so they can see it for what it is. As Area Director for Spanish Town, George is one of the most important people in Providence Road — not because of what he knows, but because of who he is and how honestly he is willing to share it.

*The following is adapted from an interview with George Gillam.*

---

## Growing Up in Louisiana

**What was the environment like when you were growing up?**

*Violent. Violent, and very dysfunctional, and hopeless. Hopeless is the best word.*

**Do you feel like the primary cause of that was due to your environment or circumstances?**

*It was due to the environment and the circumstances in the home. Most homes are a direct reflection of their community, especially in the inner city.*

**A lot of our guys in Providence Road struggle with feeling like they have limited options. Did you feel limited in your choices?**

*Choices were very limited. There were not a lot of choices. For example, in the inner city, you either fight or you don't go outside. That's the type of choice you had. You've got to fight if you want to go swim, so if you don't want to fight, you can't go swim. It's either you do or you don't — you don't have a lot of options.*

**If you were to look back, what would you say the driving motivation was during that time for you?**

*I guess my motivation was just a drive to survive. I don't think anyone wants to die, so that self-preservation kicks in, and you just want to survive. That's primarily what I found myself doing — not living, but just surviving.*

---

## The Change

**What was the primary cause for a change?**

*My change is solely and only because of Christ. I mean, that's it — because of Christ.*

George's path through incarceration and back into the community is not a success story in any easy sense. It is a story of a life that could have ended differently — that statistically should have — finding its way to a different purpose. That journey is exactly what makes George irreplaceable in Spanish Town.

---

## Disrupting the Street Code

**When you work with youth in Spanish Town, what are you trying to contrast?**

*The ideology of the street code in general. The myth of it. I think they live in a different world that is somewhat bubble-fied from the general population. I'm always trying to downplay the street code of ethics — which is immoral, which is very fake, and very false. I hold it up to the light and let them see it for what it is.*

George's ability to do this is not theoretical. When he describes the street code as false, these young men cannot dismiss him. He lived it. He knows the seductions it offers and the toll it extracts. His words carry a weight that no curriculum or outside expert can replicate.

**What are the major differences in culture that Providence Road represents, in direct opposition to what these guys are used to?**

*Number one is the willingness to serve. To serve is something you're not taught in the streets — it's seen as a sign of weakness. So when we tell the guys to be strong instead of tough, because being tough is for yourself and being strong is for others, those two things are totally contradictory to what they're accustomed to in the streets versus what they get in Providence Road.*

> "Being tough is for yourself and being strong is for others."
>
> — George Gillam, Area Director, Spanish Town

---

## The Investment Case

**If you had one thing to say to someone looking to support Providence Road, what would you tell them?**

*I would tell them that, at some point, an investment is going to be made in these guys' lives. The question is when and how much it's going to cost.*

*If you make the investment early, the investment will be less, and the dividend will be very high. But to invest later means basically you will be paying taxes for feeding and clothing and housing them inside of prison. And then there's no telling what choices these guys will make that will potentially land them inside of a jail cell — and hopefully, there's not a victim associated with their crime.*

*From zero to sixteen, I had not been invested in until someone had been fatally wounded. Then, after I was in jail, the system invested hundreds of thousands of dollars — probably millions — keeping me locked up and offering me training. I could have been a law-abiding, taxpaying citizen. So at some point, you're going to have to invest in these young guys. The question is how much are you going to pay and when are you going to pay it.*

> "Providence Road is an investment in their lives today that gives these guys options and the chance at a different future."
>
> — George Gillam

*These guys are going to get your attention one way or another. It's inevitable.*
