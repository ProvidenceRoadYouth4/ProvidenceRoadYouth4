Volunteering with Providence Road is simple in concept and profound in practice: you show up. You come to the neighborhoods of North Baton Rouge on Tuesday evenings, you work alongside youth ages 10–16, and you let consistency do what it does best — build trust. No credential is required, no specialized background needed. The Jump Team is made up of ordinary people who decided to get into the struggle, and the youth they serve have changed because of it. More often than not, the volunteers change just as much.

> "The best thing about Providence Road isn't the impact we make or the opportunities we provide, it's the people who are involved."
>
> — Cole Erno, Co-Founder

## What Volunteering Looks Like

Providence Road's weekly rhythm gives volunteers multiple ways to show up and serve:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Tuesday Service Projects" icon="trash" href="/get-involved/impact">
    Join youth for community cleanup in North Baton Rouge neighborhoods — trash removal, vacant lot clearing, and block-level restoration that youth earn the right to lead.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Wednesday Book Club" icon="book-open" href="/get-involved/impact">
    Sit with youth through the struggle of reading and understanding. Books like *The Narrative of Frederick Douglass* and *Treasure Island* spark curiosity that changes how youth see themselves.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Gym & Fitness Trips" icon="dumbbell" href="/get-involved/impact">
    Youth who build a strong track record through service earn access to gym trips. Volunteers make these possible by showing up as adults who invest in more than cleanup.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Out-of-Town Service Trips" icon="map" href="/get-involved/impact">
    The most competitive opportunity Providence Road offers. Youth earn these trips — to places like Camp Blessing in Texas and camps in Oregon — through a demonstrated track record of showing up strong.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Why Consistency Is the Most Valuable Thing You Can Offer

The youth Providence Road serves have often experienced adults as unreliable — people who show up for a season and disappear. What reshapes that narrative isn't a program; it's a person who keeps coming back.

> "We show up to serve youth, just to realize when we get down in it, that it's as much for our hearts as it is for theirs."
>
> — Coach J' (Jon Odenwald), Co-Founder & Executive Director

Mentorship in Providence Road doesn't happen through a formal curriculum. It happens in the truck on the way to a service project. It happens when a youth notices that you showed up again, and again, and again. The relationship is the program.

<Note>
  You don't need special skills or experience. You need to be willing to show up, get in the struggle, and trust the process.
</Note>

## How to Get Started

<Steps>
  <Step title="Reach Out by Email">
    Send a message to [providenceroad.jtf@gmail.com](mailto:providenceroad.jtf@gmail.com) and let the Jump Team know you're interested in volunteering. Mention what drew you to Providence Road and what nights or activities work best for you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect with the Jump Team">
    Someone from the team will follow up to walk you through upcoming service opportunities, answer your questions, and help you understand what to expect on your first evening.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Show Up for Your First Tuesday">
    Meet the youth and volunteers on a Tuesday evening service project in North Baton Rouge. Come ready to work, ready to listen, and ready to be surprised by how much a few hours can matter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep Showing Up">
    This is where volunteering becomes mentorship. The youth notice who comes back. Your consistent presence is the most powerful thing you bring to the struggle.
  </Step>
</Steps>
