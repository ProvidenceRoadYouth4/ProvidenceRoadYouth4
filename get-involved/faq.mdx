Whether you're a parent wondering if Providence Road is right for your child, a teacher curious about bringing Big Steps™ into your classroom, a potential volunteer figuring out where to start, or simply someone who wants to understand the work — this page has you covered. Providence Road operates from a simple premise: consistent presence, genuine relationships, and meaningful challenge change the trajectory of at-risk youth. These questions will help you understand what that looks like in practice and how you can be part of it.

## About the Organization

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What is Providence Road?">
    Providence Road is a Baton Rouge nonprofit founded in 2018 by Jon Odenwald (Coach J') and Cole Erno. The organization's mission is to guide, nurture, and strengthen the hearts and minds of youth in poverty — specifically Black youth ages 10–16 in under-resourced neighborhoods of North Baton Rouge. Programming includes weekly Tuesday service projects, Wednesday book clubs, gym trips, mentorship, and earned out-of-town experiences. Providence Road also offers two formal school-based programs: Big Steps™ and Chasing Understanding®.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Who does Providence Road serve?">
    Providence Road primarily serves Black youth ages 10–16 in under-resourced neighborhoods of North Baton Rouge — including communities like Spanish Town and surrounding areas. The organization specifically prioritizes youth who don't fit traditional program models: young people who aren't showing up to existing programs not because they lack potential, but because those programs weren't built with them in mind. Providence Road goes to these youth rather than waiting for them to find their way to a program.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is Providence Road a religious organization?">
    Providence Road is faith-informed — its founders and team operate from a place of personal faith, and the organization's name reflects a belief in providence. However, its formal programs — Big Steps™ and Chasing Understanding® — are entirely secular and school-compatible. These programs can be adopted by any public school, charter school, or youth-serving organization regardless of religious affiliation. Providence Road's neighborhood programming is similarly open to all youth regardless of faith background.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How is Providence Road funded?">
    Providence Road is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit funded through individual donations, recurring monthly contributors, and organizational partnerships. There is no cost to youth or families for core programming. The organization relies on its community of supporters to fund meals, service project supplies, out-of-town trip logistics, book club materials, and staff coordination. You can give a one-time gift at providence-road.org/donate or set up a monthly contribution via PayPal.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Getting Involved

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How do I volunteer?">
    The simplest path is to email [providenceroad.jtf@gmail.com](mailto:providenceroad.jtf@gmail.com) and let the Jump Team know you want to get involved. From there, someone will connect with you about upcoming Tuesday evening service projects in North Baton Rouge. You don't need any special skills or background — just the willingness to show up consistently. Consistency is the single most valuable thing a volunteer brings to the work.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I donate?">
    You can make a one-time gift at [providence-road.org/donate](https://providence-road.org/donate), or set up a recurring monthly contribution through PayPal. Monthly giving is especially valuable because it allows Providence Road to plan programming, maintain consistency for youth, and avoid the disruption of unpredictable funding cycles. Every dollar goes toward youth programming in North Baton Rouge.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can my organization partner with Providence Road?">
    Yes — organizational partnerships are an integral part of how Providence Road scales its work. Organizations like Heritage Ranch have joined Tuesday service projects and brought their own youth alongside Providence Road's, creating cross-community relationships neither group could have built alone. The Louisiana Stormwater Coalition co-led a multi-month Capitol Lake cleanup that culminated in Providence Road youth attending the State Legislature. To start a conversation about partnership, email [providenceroad.jtf@gmail.com](mailto:providenceroad.jtf@gmail.com).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I register a youth?">
    You can register a young person online at [providence-road.org/register](https://providence-road.org/register), or reach out directly by email at [providenceroad.jtf@gmail.com](mailto:providenceroad.jtf@gmail.com). Providence Road serves youth ages 10–16 in North Baton Rouge. There is no cost to youth or families for core programming. Youth do not need to be well-behaved or academically stable to start — Providence Road meets them where they are.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## School Programs

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Are Big Steps™ and Chasing Understanding® really free?">
    Yes. Both programs are currently offered free of charge. The only cost to your school or organization is the cost of any incentives you choose to incorporate (for example, small celebration items for milestone moments). Training for facilitators is included at no cost. Providence Road provides a facilitator guide, Step Log templates, and coaching support through implementation. For questions, email [providenceroad.jtf@gmail.com](mailto:providenceroad.jtf@gmail.com).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need to be affiliated with Providence Road to use these programs in my school?">
    No. Big Steps™ and Chasing Understanding® are designed to be adopted by any school — public, charter, or private — regardless of any affiliation with Providence Road as an organization. The programs are secular, school-compatible, and built to work within existing advisory periods, homeroom blocks, after-school programs, or team settings. Many schools run both programs independently after an initial onboarding and a few weeks of coaching support.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How quickly will we see results from Big Steps™ or Chasing Understanding®?">
    Many schools report visible change within **2–4 weeks**: more positive peer-to-peer language, fewer power struggles between students and teachers, and students beginning to name and build on their own progress. The changes are often small at first — a student asking for help for the first time, a peer giving a specific and genuine affirmation — but they compound quickly because the programs build cultural memory into the group.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Are these programs trauma-informed?">
    Yes. Both Big Steps™ and Chasing Understanding® are designed with trauma-sensitivity at their core. Students always control their own disclosure — no one is required to share more than they are ready to share. Affirmations are opt-in. Adults in the program model consent, repair, and healthy boundaries. Students who are averse to public attention can participate through written affirmations, small-group circles, or one-to-one recognition. The programs are structured so that no student has to speak or be spotlighted to be included.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## The Philosophy

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title='What does "Tough to Strong" mean?'>
    "Tough" and "strong" are often used interchangeably, but Providence Road draws a sharp distinction. **Tough is for yourself** — it's the self-protective posture required to survive in environments where trust is scarce and vulnerability is dangerous. Toughness is the social currency of the street. **Strong is for others** — it's the capacity to serve, to affirm, to show up for your community even when it costs you something. Providence Road's entire program model is oriented around one goal: helping youth make the shift from tough to strong. As George Gillam, Providence Road's Community Liaison, puts it: "Being strong and serving is totally contradictory to what they are accustomed to in the streets versus what they get in Providence Road."
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why service projects? Why not sports, tutoring, or something more traditional?">
    Service projects do something that sports and tutoring don't: they put youth in the position of giving rather than receiving. This is a radical shift for young people who have grown up in survival mode, where every resource — including trust — is scarce. When a youth picks up trash in his own neighborhood, he is practicing the idea that he can receive far more from his community than he can ever take. Service also builds the kind of relationships that can't be manufactured: side-by-side, common-aim work that earns trust faster than almost any other activity. And it gives youth a concrete, visible track record — a way to demonstrate strength that the street doesn't provide.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the NVITES Arc™?">
    The NVITES Arc™ is Providence Road's core engagement framework, used in both Big Steps™ and Chasing Understanding®. The six steps are: **Notice, Value, Inquire, Trade, Enjoy, Share**. The arc is designed to make growth visible, make confusion safe, and create a repeatable practice for stepping into the unknown — whether that's a confusing passage in a book, a first-time apology, or an unfamiliar experience. Learn more about how the NVITES Arc™ works inside the formal programs on the [approach page](/approach/nvites-arc).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How is Providence Road different from other youth programs?">
    Three things set Providence Road apart from most youth-serving organizations:

    **Proximity.** Providence Road goes to youth — into their streets, their neighborhoods, their daily environment — rather than asking youth to travel to a program. This removes the access barrier that keeps the hardest-to-reach youth out of every other program.

    **The Cultivation Model.** Providence Road's five-stage engagement framework meets youth at whatever stage they're in — from survival mode all the way through to stepping into community leadership. There's no minimum behavior requirement to start, and no ceiling on how far a committed youth can go.

    **The Tough to Strong framework.** Most programs try to manage behavior. Providence Road aims to change identity — from someone who is tough (self-protective, reactive, isolated) to someone who is strong (service-oriented, reliable, community-rooted). That shift, when it happens, doesn't just change how a young person acts in the program. It changes how they act at home, at school, and in their neighborhood.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
