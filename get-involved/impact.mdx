Since 2018, Providence Road has shown up in the neighborhoods of North Baton Rouge — not occasionally, not seasonally, but week after week, year after year. The result is a cumulative record that speaks for itself: tons of trash removed from streets and vacant lots, thousands of meals shared with youth, hundreds of service hours logged by young people who were told they had nothing to offer, and lives quietly but unmistakably changed by the consistency of adults who refused to stop showing up. These numbers are real. But they are also incomplete — they can't measure the moment a youth chooses to affirm a peer instead of compete with one, or the confidence a young man carries home from his first out-of-state service trip.

<Note>
  These numbers represent more than cleanup — they represent hours of relationship-building, meals shared, and youth choosing strength over toughness.
</Note>

## 2023 Summer Highlights

Summer 2023 marked Providence Road's **fifth completed year of formal programming** — a milestone celebrated with its most active season yet.

| Metric | 2023 Result |
|--------|------------|
| Youth Impacted | 75+ |
| Vacant Lots Cleaned | 200 |
| Gym Trips | 150 |
| Love the Boot Week — Trash Removed (1.5 hrs) | 6,650 lbs (3+ tons) |

**Love the Boot Week** — a special one-day community event — brought Providence Road youth and outside volunteers together for an intensive neighborhood cleanup. In an hour and a half, the group removed over 3 tons of trash from North Baton Rouge, demonstrating what mobilized youth and willing adults can accomplish when they work together.

One Providence Road senior member, CJ, earned a three-week solo trip to work on a camp service crew in Oregon — his first time out of state alone. He returned with new confidence and a determination that the entire Providence Road community felt.

## Capitol Lake Partnership

One of the most visible civic partnerships in Providence Road's history was the collaboration with the **Louisiana Stormwater Coalition** on the restoration of Capitol Lake. Over a sustained multi-month engagement, Providence Road youth and coalition volunteers accomplished what years of neglect had made seem impossible:

- **328 tires** removed from the lake and surrounding wetlands
- **2 toilets, a couch, 2 boats**, and numerous large items hauled out
- **50 bags of litter** collected from the shoreline and wetland areas
- **1,200 total bags of litter** removed across the full Coalition cleanup period

The partnership extended beyond physical cleanup. Providence Road youth accompanied Coalition leader Marie Constantin to the **State Legislature**, where they witnessed a resolution to study stormwater litter-capture funding — sponsored by Senator Franklin Foil — proposed and **passed**. For youth from North Baton Rouge, seeing themselves as agents of civic change at the state level is exactly the kind of horizon-expanding experience that defines the later stages of Providence Road's Cultivation Model.

## What the Numbers Tell You — and What They Don't

The metrics above document real, measurable impact. But the most important outcomes of Providence Road resist quantification. They show up in small moments:

- A youth named Kevin, 11 years old, turning unprompted to a peer after a service project and offering up his earned front seat in the truck because the other kid had worked harder that day.
- Kyree, age 13, writing a poem at Wednesday book club that begins: *"Worthy of happening, working towards success..."*
- An entire group of youth deciding — together — that they felt safe enough to admit they didn't understand what they were reading, and choosing to keep going anyway.

These are the outcomes Providence Road is built for. The tons of trash are evidence. The changed hearts are the point.
