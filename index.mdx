## What We've Learned About Making Students Care

After ten years and 1,500+ sessions with youth in North Baton Rouge — many of whom were written off by systems that couldn't reach them — Providence Road has developed a clear answer:

**Students don't care because no one has made caring feel safe, visible, or worth it.**

The youth we work with are running a completely rational operating system. In their environment, showing effort is risky. Being seen trying and failing is dangerous. "Tough" — which means self-protective, guarded, and hard — is the social currency that keeps them safe.

Our job isn't to fix them. It's to build an environment where **strong** — which means showing up for others — becomes the more attractive option.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start with proximity">
    We go to them. We meet youth in their neighborhoods, at their pace, on their terms. Proximity builds the trust that makes everything else possible.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make effort visible">
    Through our Big Steps™ practice, every small act of courage gets named, celebrated, and remembered. When effort becomes visible, students start making more of it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Expand their world">
    We take youth on service trips, to the state legislature, to camps in Texas and Oregon. New environments reveal new possibilities — and new identities.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hand them the mic">
    The goal is never compliance. It's ownership. When Kevin (age 11) handed his front-seat reward to a teammate who earned it more, that was the real win.
  </Step>
</Steps>
