Big Steps™ — FAQ What is Big Steps? Big Steps™ is a student-led, peer-affirmation practice that makes personal growth visible, named, and valued. Students recognize “first-time” actions (a Big Step) and affirm one another using a simple ritual built on the NVITES Arc™: Notice, Value, Inquire, Trade, Enjoy, Share. What counts as a “Big Step”? Any meaningful, effort-based first: asking for help, apologizing, turning work in on time all week, stepping back from conflict, inviting someone new, sticking with a hard task, or speaking up respectfully. How does a session run? 1. A student names a Big Step. 2. Peers give specific, authentic affirmation (the Trail Mix Role™). 3. Group recalls prior steps to build cultural memory. 4. Quick responsibility check (“ready for the next step?”). 5. Close with one NVITES action to carry into the day. How long does it take? ● Daily touch-in: 8–12 minutes. ● Weekly circle: 30–45 minutes. Works during advisory, homeroom, after-school, or team time. Who is it for? Grades 4–12; especially effective with students who are easily distracted, avoidant, or “over school.” Mixed-ability groups work well. What makes it different from points or PBIS? No tickets or prizes. Big Steps centers peer recognition + identity growth, not compliance. It builds durable motivation, not just short-term behavior. -- 1 of 3 -- Why does it work? It trains: growth mindset, SEL (empathy, belonging), distress tolerance (holding vulnerability), executive function (self-regulation), and purpose (linking actions to identity). Is it trauma-informed? Yes. Students control disclosure, affirmations are opt-in, and adults model consent, boundaries, and repair. Does it “reward bad behavior”? No. We name effortful, prosocial firsts—not the problem itself. The focus is on what the student did differently and how to repeat it. What if a student hates attention? Offer options: small-group circles, written affirmations, or one-to-one recognition. Students never have to speak to be included. How big are groups and who leads? 10–20 students; 1–2 adults facilitate while students run roles (timekeeper, affirmer, historian). The goal is youth ownership. What do we need to get started? A facilitator guide, a simple Step Log (paper or digital), and a quiet corner. No special tech required. How does Big Steps fit with academics? Pairs well with ELA (reflection, narrative), advisory/SEL blocks, and coaching/athletics. Gains in focus and belonging tend to spill over into coursework. Can families be involved? Yes. Send brief “step notes” home; invite families to add affirmations. Family recognition accelerates momentum. What training do adults need? A 2–3 hour onboarding + practice circle, then 3–4 weeks of short coaching huddles. Most staff are confident by week two. What does implementation cost? Only the cost of incentives. For now, training is free! Is Big Steps a religious program? No. Big Steps is a secular, school-compatible practice developed by Providence Road, a nonprofit serving North Baton Rouge. How soon will we see change? Often within 2–4 weeks: more positive peer talk, fewer power struggles, and students naming/stacking their own steps. -- 2 of 3 -- What about privacy and consent? We avoid sensitive details in public logs. Students choose whether a step is shared, and adults protect confidentiality. Can we customize it? Yes—roles, prompts, and reflection tools can be adapted while keeping the core protocol (naming → affirmation → recall → readiness → NVITES action). Ready to pilot? What’s the first move? 1. Pick a group and a 10-minute daily or 30-minute weekly slot. 2. Train facilitators. 3. Run Big Steps for 4 weeks and track a few metrics. 4. Review, adjust, expand.
