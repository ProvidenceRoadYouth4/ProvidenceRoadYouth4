Chasing Understanding — FAQ What is Chasing Understanding (CU)? Chasing Understanding® is a structured reading practice that teaches students to notice confusion, name it out loud, and work through it together. It flips the norm from “pretend you understand” to “own the moment you don’t,” using the NVITES Arc™: Notice, Value, Inquire, Trade, Enjoy, Share. What problem does CU solve? Many students “fake read.” CU interrupts that habit by making metacognition visible and socially safe, so real comprehension can happen. How does a CU session run? 1. Notice a confusion point in the text. 2. Value the pause (“this matters”). 3. Inquire with specific questions. 4. Trade the guess/shortcut for an honest attempt to resolve (text evidence, peer help, teacher cue). 5. Enjoy the partial win. 6. Share what unlocked understanding for others. How long does it take? ● Daily bell-ringer: 8–12 minutes. ● Weekly deep dive: 30–45 minutes. Works in ELA, intervention blocks, or after-school. Who is it for? Grades 6–12 (strong in 8–11). Especially helpful for reluctant readers, multilingual learners, and students who “perform school” without engaging. -- 1 of 4 -- How is CU different from regular reading instruction? It trains the moments of struggle (where comprehension fails), not just end-of-chapter answers. Students practice naming confusion, asking better questions, and choosing better fix-up moves. Why does CU work? ● Builds metacognition and question quality. ● Creates cultural safety for not-knowing. ● Increases time on meaningful text and evidence use. ● Turns peers into co-teachers. Is it trauma-informed? Yes. CU normalizes vulnerability, uses opt-in sharing, sentence stems, and teacher modeling. Students control what they disclose; adults protect boundaries. What if students are shy or anxious? Options: mark confusion silently (sticky note/emoji code), pair-share before whole group, or submit questions anonymously. Students can still fully participate. Group size & who leads? 10–25 students; one adult facilitator. Students take roles (timekeeper, evidence tracker, “question lifter”) to build ownership. What materials do we need? A common text (print or digital), sticky notes or a shared doc, sentence stems for questions, and a CU Log to track confusion points and resolutions. How do we measure growth? Track CU’s 4 Core Behaviors: 1. Metacognitive Pause Recognition (Did they catch the moment?) 2. Confusion Articulation (Is it clear/specific?) 3. Question Depth (Are questions improving over time?) 4. Resolution Source (Do they move from guessing to text/peer/strategy?) Plus light indicators: time-on-task, rereads with evidence, fewer “I don’t knows.” -- 2 of 4 -- How does CU fit with grades/standards? Directly supports citing evidence, vocabulary in context, monitoring comprehension, and collaborative discussion standards. Teachers can grade process artifacts (CU Logs, question quality) or keep it ungraded to encourage risk-taking. Does CU take away from content? It accelerates content learning by reducing fake reading and repeated re-teaching. Many schools see more independent reading time and faster clarification cycles. Can families be involved? Yes. Send short “what unlocked it” notes home, or share a one-page guide for parents to use the same CU stems during homework. What training do adults need? A 2–3 hour onboarding to learn the protocol, plus brief weekly huddles for the first month. Most teachers feel fluent by week two. How soon will we see change? Often within 2–4 weeks: more honest questions, more text evidence, and fewer disengaged “glaze-over” moments. What does implementation cost? Minimal. CU uses existing texts and simple tools (logs, stems). Providence Road offers starter kits, coaching, and data templates scaled to site size. Is CU a religious program? No. CU is secular and school-compatible, created by Providence Road, a nonprofit serving North Baton Rouge. Can we customize CU? Yes—keep the core sequence (Notice → Value → Inquire → Trade → Enjoy → Share) and adapt stems, roles, or logs for your context (ELA, science reading, social studies). Ready to pilot? First steps: 1. Pick one class and a 10-minute daily CU slot. 2. Train staff; post question stems; print CU Logs. 3. Run for 4 weeks and track the 4 Core Behaviors. 4.
